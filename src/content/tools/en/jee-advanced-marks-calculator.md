## Understanding the JEE Advanced Scoring System

The Joint Entrance Examination Advanced (JEE Advanced), conducted by one of the seven zonal Indian Institutes of Technology (IIT Kharagpur, IIT Bombay, IIT Madras, IIT Delhi, IIT Kanpur, IIT Roorkee, or IIT Guwahati) under the guidance of the Joint Admission Board (JAB), is India's most intellectually rigorous undergraduate engineering admission test.

Unlike examinations with fixed structures, JEE Advanced frequently varies its total marks, question types, and partial marking distributions from year to year. Both Paper 1 (morning shift) and Paper 2 (afternoon shift) are mandatory, and a candidate's Common Rank List (CRL) rank depends entirely on the combined aggregate score.

### Structure of Paper 1 and Paper 2

Each paper comprises three distinct parts:
1. **Physics**
2. **Chemistry**
3. **Mathematics**

Within each subject, questions fall into several categories:
- **Single Correct MCQs**: Typically $+3$ for correct, $-1$ for incorrect.
- **One or More Correct MCQs**: Full marks ($+4$) if all correct options are chosen without errors; partial marks ($+3$, $+2$, $+1$) if a subset of correct choices is marked without any wrong option; $-2$ if any incorrect option is selected.
- **Numerical Value Questions**: $+3$ or $+4$ marks for exact decimals/integers; generally $0$ negative marks.
- **Match the Following / Paragraph Questions**: Varied schemes, occasionally carrying negative penalties.

$$\text{Aggregate Marks} = \sum (\text{Paper 1 Subject Scores}) + \sum (\text{Paper 2 Subject Scores})$$

### Step-by-Step Worked Demonstration

Consider an aspirant who evaluates both papers against the official answer keys released by the organizing IIT:

#### Paper 1 Performance:
- **Physics**: $+42$ (correct) $- 4$ (negatives) = **38 marks**
- **Chemistry**: $+46$ (correct) $- 4$ (negatives) = **42 marks**
- **Mathematics**: $+39$ (correct) $- 4$ (negatives) = **35 marks**
- **Paper 1 Subtotal**: $38 + 42 + 35 = \mathbf{115\text{ marks}}$

#### Paper 2 Performance:
- **Physics**: $+38$ (correct) $- 4$ (negatives) = **34 marks**
- **Chemistry**: $+42$ (correct) $- 2$ (negatives) = **40 marks**
- **Mathematics**: $+35$ (correct) $- 4$ (negatives) = **31 marks**
- **Paper 2 Subtotal**: $34 + 40 + 31 = \mathbf{105\text{ marks}}$

#### Aggregate Composite Result:
$$\text{Total Score} = 115 + 105 = \mathbf{220\text{ marks}}$$

With 220 marks in a 360-mark paper (~61.1%), the candidate comfortably surpasses both individual subject cutoffs (securing $> 30$ marks per subject) and the overall qualifying threshold, typically achieving a CRL rank within the top 800 to 1,500 nationwide, securing admission to top IITs.

### Subject-Wise Cutoff Criteria

To be included in the rank list, candidates must satisfy both:
1. **Minimum Percentage in Each Subject**: Typically 8% to 10% per subject for general category candidates.
2. **Minimum Aggregate Percentage**: Typically 30% to 35% across all three subjects combined.

If a candidate secures 300 marks overall but misses the individual cutoff in Mathematics by even 1 mark, they are disqualified from the common rank list.
