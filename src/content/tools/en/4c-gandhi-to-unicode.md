## What Is the 4C Gandhi Font?

**4C Gandhi** (sometimes written Gandhi_D, Gandhi, or 4C_Gandhi) was developed by **4C Software, Ahmedabad** as a solution for Gujarati typing on early Windows PCs. It became the de-facto standard for Gujarati text in:

- **Gujarat state government** — district collectors, panchayati raj offices, revenue departments
- **Gujarat High Court and district courts** — judicial orders and cause lists
- **Regional Gujarati newspapers** — Sandesh, Divya Bhaskar, and many district-level papers
- **Educational institutions** — Gujarat University and affiliated colleges

The font encodes the Gujarati script as ASCII characters using a Remington Gujarati typewriter layout—identical in philosophy to how Kruti Dev works for Hindi, but targeting Gujarati Unicode block characters.

## Why Is My Gandhi Output Gibberish?

**1. File is Devanagari Gandhi, not Gujarati Gandhi**
Some variants of the "Gandhi" font name actually refer to Hindi/Devanagari variants, not Gujarati. If the output contains Devanagari characters (अ, क, etc.) instead of Gujarati (અ, ક, etc.), the source text may be a Devanagari Gandhi file. Contact us if you need a Devanagari-specific Gandhi converter.

**2. Gujarati special characters not in the Gandhi set**
Rare Gujarati characters like ળ, ઑ, or extended conjuncts may not be in the Gandhi 010 character set. These will appear as their underlying ASCII characters or be skipped.

**3. Mixed Gujarati and English content**
If the source file mixes Gandhi-encoded Gujarati with English text, the English portions will convert through the map and may produce unexpected Gujarati characters. Separate the Gujarati and English sections before converting.

**4. PDF copy-paste encoding loss**
Copying Gandhi text from a PDF (especially scanned or print-based PDFs) may corrupt the ASCII sequence. Try copying from the original .doc or .txt file for best results.

**5. Gandhi variant differences**
4C Software released several Gandhi variants (Gandhi, Gandhi_D, 4C_Gandhi). All share the core mapping, but minor glyph differences exist. This converter uses the standard 4C Gandhi mapping.
